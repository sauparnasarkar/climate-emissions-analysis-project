# Country NDCs and the Platform's Featured Countries

> Part of the [Functional Series](../README.md). Each country's Paris commitment is its NDC. This
> document sets out the NDCs of the platform's ten featured countries, UNEP's assessment of
> whether current policies reach the 2030 targets, and where the platform's own data sit against
> them. **The comparisons are indicative**: NDC targets and the platform's series differ in scope,
> and the differences are large for several countries (§3).
>
> **As of:** UNEP *Emissions Gap Report 2025* (NDCs and announcements to 30 September 2025;
> published November 2025); platform data to 2024. Later submissions are not reflected.

## 1. What each country has pledged

From UNEP's *Emissions Gap Report 2025*, Table 3.1 (NDCs and announced 2035 pledges as of
30 September 2025). Germany is covered by the European Union's collective NDC.

| Country | 2030 NDC | 2035 NDC or announced pledge |
|---|---|---|
| **China** | Peak CO₂ before 2030; cut CO₂ per unit of GDP 65% from 2005; non-fossil fuels about 25% of primary energy; 1,200 GW wind and solar | Cut net GHG emissions **7–10% from peak levels** by 2035, "striving to do better"; non-fossil share over 30%; wind and solar to 3,600 GW |
| **United States** | −50–52% from 2005 | −61–66% from 2005 (UNEP states that the US NDC would no longer be in effect from 27 January 2026, as the US is withdrawing) |
| **India** | Cut GHG per unit of GDP 45% from 2005; about 40% non-fossil power capacity (conditional on finance); additional 2.5–3 GtCO₂e carbon sink | No new NDC submitted or announced by 30 September 2025 |
| **Russia** | GHG up to 70% of the 1990 level | 65–67% of the 1990 level |
| **Japan** | −46% from 2013 | −60% from 2013 (and −73% by 2040) |
| **European Union (Germany)** | At least −55% net from 1990 | Announced: −66–72% net from 1990 |
| **Brazil** | −53% net from 2005 | −59–67% net from 2005 |
| **United Kingdom** | At least −68% from 1990 | At least −81% from 1990 |
| **South Africa** | Limit 2030 emissions to 350–420 MtCO₂e | No new NDC submitted or announced by 30 September 2025 |
| **Australia** | −43% from 2005 | −62–70% from 2005 |

Three features of these pledges matter for comparison:

- **Different base years** (1990, 2005, 2013, or "peak") and **different metrics**: absolute
  reductions, a percentage of a base level, an emission-intensity target (China, India), or a
  range of emissions (South Africa).
- **Net or gross.** "Net" targets count removals by forests and land, which can be large
  (Brazil, Australia, Russia, the United States).
- **Unconditional or conditional**: some pledges depend on international finance (India's
  non-fossil capacity target, South Africa's 2030 range).

## 2. Are current policies enough to reach the 2030 targets?

UNEP assesses, from several independent modelling studies, whether current policies reach each
country's **unconditional 2030** target. The bracket gives the number of studies projecting that
it will be met out of those considered.

| Country | Assessment | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| China | Likely to meet | 6 of 7 |
| European Union | Likely to meet (but see below) | 2 of 3 |
| India | Likely to meet | 4 of 5 |
| Russia | Likely to meet; projected about 25% below its target level | 3 of 3 |
| South Africa | Likely to meet its (conditional) range | 2 of 4 |
| Japan | Less likely; "very close" | 1 of 4 |
| Australia | Less likely; "very close" (its own projection agrees) | 1 of 3 |
| Brazil | Less likely | 0 of 3 |
| United Kingdom | Less likely (official UK projections also do not expect to meet the target) | 0 of 2 |
| United States | Less likely | 0 of 2 |

UNEP counts seven G20 members as likely to meet their 2030 targets and nine as not; the grouping
above follows its table and text. Official projections for the EU (the European Environment
Agency), the UK and Canada show those governments do **not** expect to meet their point-in-time
2030 targets under current policies, so the EU's "likely" rests on independent studies.

**In aggregate**: G20 emissions under current policies are projected at about 35 GtCO₂e in 2030
and 33 GtCO₂e in 2035. The *implementation gap* (between the unconditional NDCs and current
policies) is about 2 GtCO₂e, rising to 3–4 if adjusted for overachievement by some countries.

Two changes since last year are material for the featured countries:

- **China's current-policy projections now show emissions peaking around 2025** and falling by
  0.3–1.4 GtCO₂e by 2030, because renewable generation is growing faster than power demand.
  Earlier projections showed growth to 2030.
- **The United States' 2030 projection rose by about 1 GtCO₂e** after policy reversals.

## 3. Where the platform's data sit

The platform's series are **gross national emissions excluding land use**
(PRIMAP-hist total GHG, whose country values are rounded to three significant figures; OWID CO₂),
and its BAU path is a statistical continuation of each
country's own CO₂ history. NDCs are mostly **net** and **include land use**, and are measured
against different base years. Treat every number below as an indication of direction and
distance, not as a target assessment.

| Country | NDC base | Observed 2024 vs base (platform GHG) | Platform BAU CO₂ vs base, 2030 / 2035 | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| China | peak | At a record high (CO₂ 12.3 Gt; GHG 15.5 Gt) | CO₂ 13.5 Gt (+10% on 2024) / 14.4 Gt | **BAU keeps rising; UNEP's policy studies show a peak around 2025.** The platform's trend model cannot see policy-driven change |
| United States | 2005 | about −17% | CO₂ −23% / −24% | Far from −50–52%; the pledge is withdrawn in any case |
| India | 2005 (intensity) | about +109% in absolute terms; at a record high | CO₂ +220% / +262% | Target is intensity, not absolute; not comparable |
| Russia | 1990 | about −33% | CO₂ −27% / −26% | Already past −30% on a gross basis |
| Japan | 2013 | about −25% | CO₂ −31% / −33% | Short of −46% (2030) on gross figures; UNEP says "very close" on net |
| Germany (EU) | 1990 | about −48% | CO₂ −52% / −57% | −52% by 2030 on BAU, short of the EU-wide −55% (net); Germany has no separate NDC |
| Brazil | 2005 (net) | about +27% gross | CO₂ +38% / +42% | **Not comparable:** Brazil's net target counts land-use emissions and removals that the platform excludes |
| United Kingdom | 1990 | about −52% | CO₂ −56% / −59% | Short of −68% (2030) |
| South Africa | absolute range | 522 MtCO₂e (2024) | CO₂ flat at about 444 Mt | Above the 350–420 range on a gross basis; scope differs |
| Australia | 2005 | about −2% | CO₂ +2% / +3% | Far from −43% gross; Australia's net figure, with its land sink, is much lower |

Observations:

- **The platform and UNEP disagree about China, for a clear reason.** The platform's BAU is a
  damped continuation of 1990–2024 CO₂ and does not know that clean-power additions are now
  outpacing demand growth. This is the clearest example of why the platform's forecasts are
  "if past trends continue", not predictions.
- **Scope decides the answer.** Where a target is net of land-use removals, comparing it with
  gross emissions overstates the distance still to go; where land use is a net source (Brazil),
  the comparison is not meaningful at all.
- **India and China are the two featured countries whose absolute emissions are still at record
  highs** (platform data). Both pledge intensity or peak-based targets, not absolute cuts from a
  past base year.
- **Having cut emissions by half since 1990 does not guarantee the next target.** Germany and the
  UK are each about 48–52% below 1990 on the platform's gross figures, yet UNEP assesses the UK
  as less likely to meet its 2030 target on current policies.

## 4. What the new NDCs add up to

- **Only 64 Parties, covering 63% of global emissions, had submitted or announced new NDCs** by
  UNEP's cut-off of 30 September 2025, against a February 2025 deadline.
- UNEP estimates full implementation of the 2035 NDCs would cut emissions about **12% (unconditional)
  to 15% (conditional) below 2019** by 2035, or 9% and 11% without the US NDC. The secretariat's
  update presented at COP30 reportedly put it at about 12% below 2019 across a larger set of
  NDCs.
- The pathway benchmarks for 2035 are **−35% (2 °C) and −55% (1.5 °C)** in UNEP's pathway set.
- The resulting warming projections span **2.3–2.5 °C** if all NDCs are fully implemented,
  against up to 2.8 °C under current policies (UNEP), with the Climate Action Tracker at 2.6 °C
  for both.
