# The Paris Targets as Numbers

> Part of the [Functional Series](../README.md). The Agreement states its goals as temperatures.
> To compare them with emissions data, they have to be turned into quantities: how much can still
> be emitted, how fast emissions must fall, and by when. This document does that translation and
> lays the platform's pathways next to it. Where the platform's own numbers are used, they come
> from the scenarios described in
> [Scenarios and Temperature Mapping](../02-methodology/04-scenarios-and-temperature-mapping.md).

## 1. The goals in numbers

| Goal | In numbers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Well below 2 °C, pursue 1.5 °C, above 1850–1900 | Paris Agreement, Article 2.1(a) |
| Where warming stands | About 1.4 °C long-term (observed 2023–2025 average about 1.5 °C) | Copernicus; Forster et al. 2026 (human-induced 1.37 °C in 2025) |
| Emissions in 2030 | GHG −43% (range −34 to −60%) and CO₂ −48% from 2019 for 1.5 °C; GHG −21% and CO₂ −22% for 2 °C | IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report, Table SPM.1 |
| Emissions in 2035 | GHG −60% and CO₂ −65% (1.5 °C); GHG −35% and CO₂ −37% (2 °C) | IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report, Table SPM.1 |
| 2035 in UNEP's pathway set | −55% (1.5 °C) and −35% (2 °C) in total GHG | UNEP *Emissions Gap Report 2025* |
| Net zero CO₂ | Early 2050s (1.5 °C); early 2070s (2 °C) | IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report B.6.1 |
| Net zero GHG | Around the 2070s in 1.5 °C pathways that reach it | IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report B.6.1 |
| Remaining carbon budget, 1.5 °C (50%) | 130 GtCO₂ (Forster et al.) or 170 GtCO₂ (Global Carbon Budget), from the start of 2026 | See [Benchmarks](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md) |
| Remaining carbon budget, 2 °C (50%) | about 1,050 GtCO₂, from the start of 2026 | Forster et al.; Global Carbon Budget 1,055 |
| Peak | GHG emissions peak between 2020 and "at the latest before 2025" in 1.5 °C pathways | IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report B.6.1 |

The 43% and 60% figures are the numbers the first global stocktake reaffirmed in 2023.

## 2. How fast would emissions have to fall?

The pathway figures are reductions from 2019. Observed emissions have since risen (fossil CO₂
was 4.1% above 2019 in 2024), so the required rate from today is steeper than the headline
percentage suggests. The table converts each CO₂ pathway into the **constant annual percentage
cut from the 2024 level** that would reach it:

| Pathway (CO₂, from 2019) | By 2030 | By 2035 | By 2040 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.5 °C** median reduction | −48% | −65% | −80% |
| Constant cut needed each year from 2024 | **10.9% a year** | 9.4% a year | 9.8% a year |
| **2 °C** median reduction | −22% | −37% | −51% |
| Constant cut needed each year from 2024 | **4.7% a year** | 4.5% a year | 4.6% a year |

(Calculated on fossil CO₂ including bunkers, 38.6 GtCO₂ in 2024 and 37.1 in 2019. Including land
use changes the rates by under 0.3 percentage points.)

For scale:

- **The largest annual fall since 1960 was in 2020, the pandemic year: −5.2%** in the platform's
  fossil CO₂ series (the IPCC gives −5.8% for CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry). The next
  largest falls were about −3.0% (1992) and −2.7% (1981).
- A **sustained** 4.5–4.7% a year, the 2 °C requirement, is roughly the 2020 pandemic fall
  repeated every year for 15 years. The 1.5 °C requirement of about 10% a year is roughly twice
  that.
- These are **median pathway** requirements. The ranges in the IPCC's table are wide (for the
  1.5 °C 2030 CO₂ reduction, −36% to −69%).

## 3. The platform's pathways against the targets

The platform's scenarios (CO₂, 2025–2040) are shown in
[Pathways](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md). In summary, by 2035 relative to 2019:

| | BAU | Moderate (−2%/yr) | Aggressive (−5%/yr) | IPCC 2 °C median | IPCC 1.5 °C median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CO₂ change by 2035 | +16% | −5% | −31% | −37% | −65% |

No platform scenario meets even the 2 °C median pathway. The platform's scenarios are
illustrative bounds, not forecasts of policy, but the arithmetic is informative. Because the
cuts are applied to a business-as-usual path that is still growing, the Aggressive scenario's
5% cut is a net fall of only about 3.8% a year between 2024 and 2040 (38.6 to 20.8 GtCO₂).
Sustained for 16 years, that is faster than every single year in the past 60 except the 2020
pandemic, and it is still short of the roughly 4.6% a year the 2 °C median requires.

## 4. When would the carbon budgets run out?

Cumulative CO₂ from the platform's scenarios (fossil and cement, plus land use held flat at
about 4.7 GtCO₂ a year) can be run against the remaining budgets. Each cell is the first year the
scenario's cumulative emissions since the start of 2026 reach the budget, at a 50% likelihood.

| Budget from the start of 2026 (Forster et al. / Global Carbon Budget) | BAU | Moderate | Aggressive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 °C: 130 / 170 GtCO₂ | 2028 / 2029 | 2029 / 2029 | 2029 / 2030 |
| 1.7 °C: 500 / 525 GtCO₂ | 2036 / 2037 | 2038 / 2038 | not by 2040 (496 emitted by 2040) |
| 2 °C: 1,050 / 1,055 GtCO₂ | not by 2040 | not by 2040 | not by 2040 |

(Each year pair reads the result under Forster et al.'s budget / the Global Carbon Budget's.) Cumulative emissions from 2026
to 2040 are 702 GtCO₂ for BAU, 608 for Moderate and 496 for Aggressive.

How to read this:

- **The 1.5 °C budget is used up around 2028–2030 in every scenario**, whichever of the two
  budget estimates is used. The difference between the 130 and 170 figures moves the date by at
  most a year, and the scenarios themselves differ by at most about two years (2028 for BAU,
  2030 for Aggressive): the budget is consumed faster than any of these pathways can bend.
- **The 1.7 °C budget is passed in the late 2030s except under the Aggressive scenario**, which
  reaches it only at the end of 2040.
- **The 2 °C budget lasts beyond 2040 in all three.**
- **Using up the budget is not the same as reaching 1.5 °C.** The published budgets include
  uncertainty in how warming responds to CO₂ only, assume a further 0.10 °C of future non-CO₂
  warming, and are for a 50% chance. The overshoot the COP30 decision acknowledged is related:
  the budget is spent before emissions reach zero.

## 5. What the targets imply for the platform's own temperature path

The platform's BAU temperature path reaches a level of 1.5 °C in 2033 and Aggressive in 2034
(see [Pathways §5](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md)). That is consistent with the
IPCC's finding that the first 20-year average to reach 1.5 °C is centred in the first half of the
2030s in all but its highest-emissions scenario, and with Forster et al.'s estimate of around
2030 for human-induced warming. The Paris goal concerns the long-term level, so a single warm
year above 1.5 °C (2024 already was) does not by itself mean the goal is breached.
