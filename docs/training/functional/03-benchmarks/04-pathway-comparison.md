# Pathways, Carbon Budgets and Warming Outlooks

> Part of the [Functional Series](../README.md). The previous documents compared the platform's
> *observations* with benchmarks. This one compares its *scenarios* with the IPCC's emission
> pathways, sets out the reference carbon budgets (where the sources disagree), and compares the
> platform's implied warming with published outlooks. What these comparisons imply for the Paris
> targets is the subject of [Paris Agreement section](../04-paris-agreement/02-targets-as-numbers.md).

## 1. Reference pathways

The IPCC's AR6 Synthesis Report (Table SPM.1) gives the emission reductions from 2019 levels
(median, with the 5th–95th percentile range) in modelled pathways that meet each goal.

| Pathway | Gas | 2030 | 2035 | 2040 | 2050 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.5 °C (>50%), no or limited overshoot** | GHG | 43 [34–60] | 60 [49–77] | 69 [58–90] | 84 [73–98] |
| | CO₂ | 48 [36–69] | 65 [50–96] | 80 [61–109] | 99 [79–119] |
| **2 °C (>67%)** | GHG | 21 [1–42] | 35 [22–55] | 46 [34–63] | 64 [53–77] |
| | CO₂ | 22 [1–44] | 37 [21–59] | 51 [36–70] | 73 [55–90] |

(Percent reduction from 2019.) UNEP's Emissions Gap Report 2025 uses its own pathway set and gives
a slightly different benchmark for 2035: reductions of **35% (range 28–43) for 2 °C and 55%
(range 50–63) for 1.5 °C**, in total GHG. The two sets are not interchangeable; the IPCC figures
are used below.

## 2. The platform's scenarios against the CO₂ pathways

The platform's scenarios cover CO₂ only, so they are compared with the **CO₂** rows. Two scope
points apply:

- IPCC CO₂ figures are **net of land use**; the platform's scenarios model fossil and cement CO₂
  and **hold land use flat**. Both variants are shown; the "including flat land use" row is
  closer to the IPCC's definition, but the IPCC pathways also reduce land-use emissions.
- The platform's global figure is its 40 covered countries scaled up by holding the rest of the
  world at its 2024 share (10.7%).

Change in CO₂ from 2019 (2019 fossil CO₂ is 37.1 GtCO₂ and total including land use 42.0).
For reference, **observed 2024 emissions were already +4.1% above 2019 (fossil) and +2.9% above
(including land use)**, before any of the scenario years below.

| | 2030 | 2035 | 2040 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Platform BAU**, fossil | +10.6% | +15.9% | +21.0% |
| BAU, incl. flat land use | +8.8% | +13.5% | +18.0% |
| **Platform Moderate (−2%/yr)**, fossil | −0.1% | −5.3% | −10.6% |
| Moderate, incl. flat land use | −0.6% | −5.2% | −9.9% |
| **Platform Aggressive (−5%/yr)**, fossil | −14.4% | −30.6% | −43.9% |
| Aggressive, incl. flat land use | −13.3% | −27.6% | −39.4% |
| *IPCC 2 °C (>67%), CO₂* | *−22%* | *−37%* | *−51%* |
| *IPCC 1.5 °C (>50%), CO₂* | *−48%* | *−65%* | *−80%* |

How to read this:

- **Observed emissions rose between 2019 and 2024**, while both IPCC pathways need falls. The
  pathways assume immediate action, which the IPCC defines as starting between 2020 and 2025.
- **Even the Aggressive scenario sits above the 2 °C median** in every year shown (for example
  −27.6% against −37% in 2035, and −39.4% against −51% in 2040), and far above the 1.5 °C median.
  This is a statement about the platform's *illustrative* scenario, not about what is achievable.
- **Moderate is roughly flat against 2019 in 2030** because its 2% a year is taken off a
  business-as-usual path that is still rising (+10.6% by 2030). A constant percentage cut
  compounds from the starting point of a growing pathway.
- **The IPCC range is wide.** For the 2 °C pathway in 2040 it runs from −36% to −70%; the
  platform's Aggressive scenario (−39% to −44%) is inside the 2 °C range but near its weak end (the smaller reductions).

## 3. Carbon budgets: where the references disagree

The remaining carbon budget is the cumulative CO₂ that can still be emitted for a given
probability of holding warming to a level. The reference values differ because they are
assessed at different dates, with different warming estimates.

| Source | Start of budget | 1.5 °C (50%) | 1.7 °C (50%) | 2 °C (50%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IPCC AR6 (Synthesis Report B.5.2) | Beginning of 2020 | 500 GtCO₂ | | 1,150 GtCO₂ (67%) |
| Forster et al. 2026 (Table 8) | Beginning of 2026 | 130 | 500 | 1,050 |
| Global Carbon Budget 2025 | Beginning of 2026 | 170 | 525 | 1,055 |

Reading across:

- **Forster et al. and the Global Carbon Budget agree on the larger budgets** (1.7 °C: 500
  against 525; 2 °C: 1,050 against 1,055) and **differ by 40 GtCO₂ on 1.5 °C** (130 against
  170). Both are small compared with the 2020 starting figure.
- **The IPCC's 500 from 2020 is not directly comparable** with the 2026 values by subtraction.
  Forster et al. put the total reduction since AR6 at about 370 GtCO₂, of which only part is
  emissions since 2020; the rest reflects revised warming estimates.
- **At 2025 emissions the 1.5 °C budget is nearly gone:** about 3 years at 42 GtCO₂ a year
  (Forster et al.) or about 4 years (Global Carbon Budget).
- **Using up the budget is not the same as reaching 1.5 °C.** Forster et al. state this
  explicitly. The values also cover only the uncertainty in the CO₂–warming relationship and
  assume a further 0.10 °C of non-CO₂ warming, which the platform does not model.

Any further figure for the 1.5 °C budget quoted from a secondary source should be checked
against these primary values; at least one popular summary of the 2026 update quoted the
67%-likelihood value (80) as if it were the central estimate.

## 4. Warming outlooks to 2100

The platform does not project temperature beyond 2040, so these cannot be compared directly with
its output. They show where the platform's short-horizon result sits in the wider picture.

| Source | Scenario | Warming by 2100 |
|---|---|---|
| IPCC AR6 WGIII | Policies implemented by end of 2020 | 3.2 [2.2–3.5] °C |
| IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report | NDCs announced before COP26, no further ambition | 2.8 [2.1–3.4] °C |
| UNEP EGR 2025 | Current policies | "up to" 2.8 °C |
| UNEP EGR 2025 | Full implementation of all NDCs | 2.3–2.5 °C |
| Climate Action Tracker (13 Nov 2025) | Current policies | 2.6 °C |
| Climate Action Tracker | 2030 and 2035 targets (NDCs) | 2.6 °C |
| Climate Action Tracker | Pledges and targets incl. net zero | 2.2 °C |
| Climate Action Tracker | Optimistic: all announced targets fully implemented | 1.9 °C |

The **two "2.8 °C" figures are different scenarios**: the IPCC's is for NDCs announced before
COP26 (2021); UNEP's is for policies in place in 2025. Estimates range from about 1.9 °C to
3.2 °C depending on what is assumed to be implemented, and UNEP notes that methodological
updates accounted for 0.1 °C of its own improvement from last year's 2.6–2.8 °C.

## 5. The platform's implied warming against published rates

| | Platform (BAU, to 2040) | Forster et al. 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Level in 2025 | 1.34 °C | 1.37 °C (human-induced) |
| Rate | about 0.23 °C a decade | 0.27 °C a decade (2016–2025) |
| Year the level reaches 1.5 °C | 2033 (BAU), 2033 (Moderate), 2034 (Aggressive) | around 2030 (at current emissions) |

The platform's rate is lower and its crossing about three years later than Forster et al.'s.
For context, the IPCC reports that the midpoint of the first 20-year average to reach 1.5 °C lies
in the first half of the 2030s in all but its highest-emissions scenario, and gives 2030–2035 as
the median five-year interval across the Working Group III pathway categories; the platform's
2033–2034 falls inside that window. Note that the IPCC's definition is a 20-year average, while
the platform's "level" is a five-year-anchored annual value, so the match is indicative. This is the direction
expected of a simplified model that uses only a CO₂ slope: it omits the additional warming from
non-CO₂ gases and from falling aerosol cooling that the published rates include. The platform's
figures should therefore be read as likely understating near-term warming, not as a central estimate.

## 6. What this comparison shows

- **The scenarios are illustrative bounding cases and fall short of Paris-aligned pathways.**
  Even the Aggressive scenario is above the 2 °C median CO₂ pathway.
- **Observed emissions have so far risen against the 2019 starting point** of the pathways.
- **Carbon budgets for 1.5 °C are nearly exhausted** under both current assessments, which
  differ by 40 GtCO₂.
- **Published outlooks span roughly 1.9–3.2 °C by 2100**, and the platform's short-horizon
  warming sits slightly below published rates.
