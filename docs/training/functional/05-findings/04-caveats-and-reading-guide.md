# Findings: Caveats and a Reading Guide

> Part of the [Functional Series](../README.md). This document closes the series. It gathers the
> uncertainties that the findings carry forward, says what the platform cannot conclude, and gives
> a short guide for quoting its results responsibly.

## 1. Uncertainties carried forward

These are open, not resolved. Each is stated where it first arose.

| Uncertainty | Why it is open | Effect on the findings |
|---|---|---|
| **China's trajectory** | The platform's BAU keeps rising to 2030 (about +10%); UNEP's policy-based studies show a peak around 2025 | The platform's forecast for China, and so for the global total, is likely too high; it is a continuation of past behaviour, not a policy projection ([Finding 12](03-country-differences.md)) |
| **Methane** | The platform's methane (8.2 GtCO₂e in 2019) is below the IPCC's (11) and UNEP's (9.3 for 2024); the cause was not traced | The platform's total greenhouse gas figure is lower than global inventories partly for an unexplained reason ([Emissions §2](../03-benchmarks/02-emissions-vs-benchmarks.md)) |
| **Cement carbonation sink** | The platform's 2024 fossil CO₂ (38.6) is about 0.7 GtCO₂ above the Global Carbon Budget (37.8), the size of the sink the latter nets out; OWID's codebook does not state its treatment | The explanation is consistent but not verified; budget crossing years come out slightly early ([Targets §4](../04-paris-agreement/02-targets-as-numbers.md)) |
| **Preliminary temperature data** | Berkeley Earth labels its high-resolution product "preliminary", and the platform's recent years differ from Berkeley's own report by up to about 0.03 °C | The latest single-year temperatures may be revised |
| **Two carbon budgets** | 130 and 170 GtCO₂ for 1.5 °C, from different assessments | Crossing years move by at most a year |
| **Land use** | About ±2.6 GtCO₂ a year (one standard deviation) and held flat in scenarios | Totals including land use are the least certain figures in the series |
| **Source texts** | The UNFCCC's own decision documents could not be retrieved for this series | The wording of the COP28 and COP30 decisions should be checked against the official text before it is quoted |

## 2. What the platform cannot conclude

- **Policy effects.** It does not model policy, prices, technology or behaviour. It cannot say
  what any pledge or law will do.
- **Temperature beyond 2040.** The horizon is 2040; the published outlooks to 2100 are other
  organisations' work.
- **Attribution to countries.** No country series is regressed against temperature, and no
  warming is assigned to a country.
- **Causation.** The emissions–temperature relationship is a statistical association over 175
  years, offered as context.
- **Whether any single pledge is fair or ambitious.** That needs a common metric the platform
  does not compute.
- **Feasibility.** That a pathway requires a given rate of cuts does not show it can or cannot
  be achieved.

## 3. How to quote this work

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Say which source, edition and scope a number comes from | Quote a total without saying whether it includes land use, bunkers, or all gases |
| Describe scenarios as *illustrative* | Describe the 2040 temperature levels as forecasts |
| Say "likely range" for the IPCC's TCRE (66%+), and "95% interval" for the platform's slope | Treat the two as the same confidence level |
| Compare the platform's trend with a benchmark on the same scope | Compare a gross figure with a net pledge and call the gap a failure |
| Use multi-year averages for the Paris temperature goal | Say the 1.5 °C limit has been "breached" on the strength of one or two years |
| Date every statement about pledges and the COP process | Present late-2025 facts as current |

## 4. One-paragraph summary

Emissions have not yet fallen from their 2019 level, and atmospheric CO₂ and temperature continue
to rise on the paths the IPCC describes. Long-term warming is about 1.4 °C, and the remaining 1.5 °C
carbon budget is almost spent: the platform's scenarios, which are illustrative, all use it up by
about 2030 and none follows a Paris-aligned pathway. The required rate of cuts (about 4.6% a year
for 2 °C, about 10% for 1.5 °C) has no precedent in the record since 1960. National pledges for
2035 would cut emissions about 12–15% from 2019, against the 35–55% that pathways require, and
published warming outlooks for 2100 range from about 1.9 to 3.2 °C depending on what is
implemented. Countries differ widely: some peaked decades ago, while China and India are at
record highs, and China is where the platform's statistical forecast is least likely to capture
policy-driven change. Every one of
these statements has limits, set out above and in the sections they come from.
