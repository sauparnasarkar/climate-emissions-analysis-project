# Findings: Where Things Stand

> Part of the [Functional Series](../README.md). This section draws conclusions from the
> platform's data and the comparisons made in the earlier sections. It adds no new analysis:
> every finding points to the table that supports it, and states what it does *not* say. Read the
> [caveats](04-caveats-and-reading-guide.md) before quoting any of it.
>
> **As of:** platform data to 2024 (emissions) and 2025 (concentration, temperature); references
> as dated in [Benchmarks](../03-benchmarks/01-reference-landscape.md) and
> [Paris Agreement](../04-paris-agreement/01-the-agreement.md).

## How each finding is written

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Finding** | A bounded statement of what the data show |
| **Evidence** | Where in this series the support is |
| **Confidence** | High, moderate or low, with the reason |
| **Does not say** | The over-reading to avoid |

## Finding 1. Global emissions have not yet started to fall

**Finding.** Fossil CO₂ was higher in 2024 than in 2019, the starting point of the IPCC
pathways: **+4.1%** on fossil CO₂ and **+2.9%** including land use. It grew about **1% to 1.3%** in 2024
across the sources that exclude land use.

**Evidence.** [Pathways §2](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md) (observed 2024 against
2019); [Emissions §3](../03-benchmarks/02-emissions-vs-benchmarks.md) (2024 growth across five
sources).

**Confidence.** High for direction. The sources agree on the sign and, once land use is set
aside, on the size within a few tenths of a percent. The platform's total greenhouse gas figure
(50.6 GtCO₂e) is lower than UNEP's (57.7) by scope, not by disagreement about the trend.

**Does not say.** That emissions will not peak soon. The platform has no view on policy, and UNEP's
policy-based studies project China's emissions peaking around 2025.

## Finding 2. Atmospheric CO₂ keeps rising at a steady pace

**Finding.** Global-mean CO₂ was **425.6 ppm in 2025**, up from 410.1 in 2019: about **2.6 ppm a
year**. The platform's Mauna Loa series, which runs about 1.7 ppm above the global mean, reads
427.4.

**Evidence.** [Concentration §4](../03-benchmarks/03-temperature-and-concentration-vs-benchmarks.md).

**Confidence.** Very high: direct measurement, and independent global means agree to within
0.05 ppm.

**Does not say.** Anything about the rate in future years, which depends on emissions and on how
well land and ocean sinks keep absorbing CO₂.

## Finding 3. The planet is about 1.4 °C warmer than pre-industrial, and the recent years are above 1.5 °C

**Finding.** The long-term (underlying) warming level is about **1.4 °C** (Copernicus; Berkeley
Earth; Forster et al.'s human-induced 1.37 °C). The platform's data give **1.45 °C for 2025 and
1.55 °C for 2024**, and **1.49 °C for the three years 2023–2025**, against single-year spreads of
about 0.1 °C between datasets.

**Evidence.** [Temperature §1–3](../03-benchmarks/03-temperature-and-concentration-vs-benchmarks.md).

**Confidence.** High that warming is about 1.3–1.4 °C and rising roughly 0.27 °C a decade
(Forster et al.). Moderate for any single year: the platform's temperature series is
preliminary, and individual years vary by about ±0.25 °C through natural variability.

**Does not say.** That the Paris 1.5 °C limit has been breached. The goal concerns the
long-term level, which the IPCC reports as a 20-year average. Several sources estimate the
underlying level will reach 1.5 °C around 2030 or in the early-to-mid 2030s.

## Finding 4. Cumulative emissions track warming closely, as the IPCC says they should

**Finding.** Across 1850–2024 the platform finds **0.486 °C of warming per 1,000 GtCO₂**
(95% interval 0.442–0.530, R² 0.89), inside the IPCC's *likely* range of 0.27–0.63. Cumulative
emissions to 2019 (2,542 GtCO₂) sit within the IPCC's estimate of 2,400 ± 240.

**Evidence.** [Relationship §2–4](../02-methodology/03-emissions-temperature-relationship.md);
[Emissions §4](../03-benchmarks/02-emissions-vs-benchmarks.md).

**Confidence.** Moderate. The agreement with the IPCC is encouraging, but the platform's figure
is an analogue that also absorbs non-CO₂ warming, uses one climate history, and drifts upward
when only recent decades are fitted (0.63 for 1970–2024).

**Does not say.** That emissions *caused* the warming on the strength of this regression alone
(the platform labels it "context, not proof"), or that any country is responsible for a share of
the warming.

## Summary

| # | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Global emissions are still above their 2019 level | High |
| 2 | CO₂ concentration rises about 2.6 ppm a year | Very high |
| 3 | Long-term warming is about 1.4 °C; the last three years averaged above 1.5 °C | High (trend); moderate (single years) |
