# Findings: The Trajectory Against the Paris Targets

> Part of the [Functional Series](../README.md). This document answers the series' central
> question: what do the platform's trends say about progress towards the Paris targets? Each
> finding is bounded and points to its evidence. The platform's scenarios are illustrative, not
> forecasts of policy; where a conclusion depends on that, it says so.

## Finding 5. The platform's pathways fall short of Paris-aligned pathways

**Finding.** By 2035, relative to 2019, the platform's CO₂ pathways reach **+16% (BAU), −5%
(Moderate) and −31% (Aggressive)**. The IPCC's median pathways need **−37% for 2 °C and −65% for
1.5 °C**. Even the Aggressive scenario is above the 2 °C median in 2030, 2035 and 2040.

**Evidence.** [Pathways §2](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md);
[Targets §3](../04-paris-agreement/02-targets-as-numbers.md).

**Confidence.** High that the *scenarios as defined* fall short (an arithmetic fact). Moderate
for what that implies: the scenarios are constant-percentage cuts applied uniformly, and a
different design could do better.

**Does not say.** That Paris-aligned pathways are impossible, or that the true path will look
like any of the three scenarios.

## Finding 6. The required pace is well beyond anything in the record since 1960

**Finding.** From the 2024 level, a constant cut of about **4.6% a year** would follow the 2 °C
median CO₂ pathway to 2035–2040, and about **9–11% a year** the 1.5 °C median. The largest
annual fall since 1960 was **5.2% in 2020**, the pandemic year; the Aggressive scenario's
net fall averages about 3.8% a year.

**Evidence.** [Targets §2–3](../04-paris-agreement/02-targets-as-numbers.md).

**Confidence.** High for the arithmetic; the IPCC ranges around each median are wide.

**Does not say.** That such cuts are infeasible. It says there is no precedent in the platform's
record since 1960 apart from the 2020 pandemic, and that was a single year.

## Finding 7. The remaining 1.5 °C carbon budget is almost spent

**Finding.** The two current assessments put the 50%-likelihood budget for 1.5 °C at **130
GtCO₂ (Forster et al.) or 170 GtCO₂ (Global Carbon Budget)** from the start of 2026: **three to
four years** at the 2025 emissions rate of about 42 GtCO₂ a year. In the platform's scenarios
the budget is used up between **2028 and 2030**, whichever estimate is used and whichever
scenario. The 2 °C budget (about 1,050 GtCO₂) lasts beyond 2040 in all three scenarios.

**Evidence.** [Pathways §3](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md);
[Targets §4](../04-paris-agreement/02-targets-as-numbers.md).

**Confidence.** Moderate to high. The two assessments differ by 40 GtCO₂ and the platform's
crossing years come out slightly early (its accounting runs about 1.3 GtCO₂ a year above the
budgets' basis), but a one-year shift does not change the conclusion.

**Does not say.** That warming reaches 1.5 °C the day the budget runs out. The budgets cover only
the CO₂–warming uncertainty and assume a further 0.10 °C of non-CO₂ warming; the platform's own
temperature level reaches 1.5 °C in 2033–2034, later than the budget, for that specific reason.

## Finding 8. The scenarios matter less by 2040 than the emissions already emitted

**Finding.** In 2040 the platform's implied warming differs by only **0.10 °C** between BAU
(1.68 °C) and Aggressive (1.58 °C), though annual emissions differ by about 2.2 times. Most
(78–83%) of the 2040 level is warming already observed by 2024.

**Evidence.** [Scenarios §5](../02-methodology/04-scenarios-and-temperature-mapping.md).

**Confidence.** Moderate. The bands cover slope uncertainty only, and the platform's rate of
warming (about 0.23 °C a decade) is below Forster et al.'s 0.27, so it likely understates
near-term warming.

**Does not say.** That mitigation does not matter. The gap widens every year the pathways stay
apart (about 0.025 °C by 2032, 0.10 °C by 2040).

## Finding 9. Pledges and policies are not yet on a Paris-aligned path

**Finding.** UNEP estimates that full implementation of the 2035 NDCs would cut emissions by
about **12% (unconditional) to 15% (conditional)** from 2019, against **35% for 2 °C and 55%
for 1.5 °C**. Projected warming this century is **2.3–2.5 °C** if all NDCs are fully
implemented, and up to **2.8 °C** on current policies (UNEP); the Climate Action Tracker puts
both at 2.6 °C and its optimistic scenario at 1.9 °C. By the Paris cycle, only 64 Parties
covering 63% of emissions had submitted or announced new NDCs by 30 September 2025.

**Evidence.** [Pathways §4](../03-benchmarks/04-pathway-comparison.md);
[Country NDCs §4](../04-paris-agreement/03-country-ndcs.md).

**Confidence.** Moderate. These are other organisations' projections, with their own ranges,
and they moved by 0.1 °C or more between editions for methodological reasons.

**Does not say.** That pledges and policies cannot improve. The Agreement's design assumes
they ratchet upward at each five-year round, and the next formal checkpoint is the 2028
global stocktake.

## Where the platform's evidence ends

| Question | What the platform can support | What needs other evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Are emissions falling? | Observed to 2024: not yet | What policies will do next |
| Is the 1.5 °C limit still within reach? | The budget is nearly spent; overshoot is likely on these pathways | Whether large removals or very rapid cuts will follow |
| What will warming be in 2100? | Nothing: the horizon is 2040 | The published outlooks (1.9–3.2 °C by 2100, depending on assumptions) |
| Which countries will meet their NDCs? | Direction and distance, on gross emissions | UNEP's policy-based assessment ([Country NDCs](../04-paris-agreement/03-country-ndcs.md)) |
