Why emissions matter
Emissions raise the concentration of CO₂ in the atmosphere (now 424.6 ppm). What accumulates increases radiative forcing, the extra heat the atmosphere holds, and that warming is observed as the temperature anomaly: +1.62 °C in 2024.
Temperature reflects accumulated global forcing and the ocean–climate system’s response, not only this year’s emissions.
These are global figures. The sections below explore emissions by country; they do not assign warming to countries.

Temperature & GHG Correlation

Causal chainLong-run relationshipRecent all-gasGas compositionCountry responsibilityScenariosMethodology & sources
This module reads the link between emissions and warming from observed data. It is descriptive: relationships are shown as context, not as proof of cause, and nothing here is a climate model.

The causal chain

#causal-chain
  1. 1 · EMISSIONSAnnual flows of greenhouse gases, in mass per year.
    On this platformOWID CO₂ (country + World); PRIMAP-hist all GHGs, 1970+
  2. →
  3. 2 · CONCENTRATIONThe amount that stays in the air: 285 ppm of CO₂ in 1850, 424.6 in 2024.
    On this platformObserved only: Law Dome + NOAA Mauna Loa. Not modelled.
  4. →
  5. 3 · RADIATIVE FORCINGExtra heat trapped because more greenhouse gas is in the air.
    On this platformConcept only. No forcing data, calculation or endpoint.
  6. →
  7. 4 · TEMPERATURE ANOMALYObserved warming against the 1850–1900 average: +1.62 °C in 2024.
    On this platformBerkeley Earth global series

Each year’s emissions add to a stock of CO₂ in the atmosphere. Roughly half of what is emitted stays there, so concentration keeps rising even when annual emissions level off. A higher concentration traps more of the heat Earth would otherwise radiate to space, and over time the surface warms.

Because warming follows the accumulated stock and is delayed by the ocean, a country’s emissions in one year do not produce a matching temperature in that year. The charts below therefore compare temperature with cumulative emissions.

Annual emissions differ 2.2× by 2040, but implied temperatures differ by only 0.11 °C. 78% of the 2040 level (1.39 °C) was already observed by 2024. Why emissions series differ
OWID/GCP and PRIMAP-hist differ in scope, method, treatment of bunker fuels and process emissions, and data vintage. Differences between them are expected.
Two global totals
The long-run relationship uses the OWID World row, which includes international aviation and shipping because they reach the atmosphere. Country shares use the sum of national emissions, so shares add to 100%.
The 1959 splice
Concentration uses Law Dome ice-core values to 1958 and Mauna Loa from 1959. The gap in the overlap years is documented in /meta and marked on every concentration chart.
